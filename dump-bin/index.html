Quick heads-up before you hit send — this isn't quite like printing at home.

Once you confirm, your files begin a suspiciously scenic journey through our byzantine stack:

  1. Payload assembly. Files get bundled into a MIME-encoded envelope and handed to the dispatch queue.
  2. SMTP relay. The envelope hops across a few pieces of corporate mail plumbing — each with its own opinion on attachment size.
  3. Fax gateway handoff. An email-to-fax bridge picks it up, looks up the store's fax number, and transcodes everything into the T.38 protocol.
  4. POTS dial-in. A decade of telephony abstractions kicks in to dial the store over a very-much-still-real phone line.
  5. Ink on paper. A long-suffering Brother MFC at the customer service desk coughs out your pages.

Total trip time is usually 30–90 seconds. It really is working, even when nothing on screen looks like it is. Please don't send twice — duplicate jobs jam the fax and waste a small forest.

    Section 1 — Welcome

    Welcome to the team.

    This document explains how the job works — what you'll be doing, how to do it well, and who to ask when something doesn't match what you expected. Read it before your first shift. Keep it after.

    1.1 — Who you work for

    You work for The Retail Odyssey Company. That's the name on your badge.

    None of this affects your job. It's here so you don't spend your first week wondering whether you were hired by two different employers.

    1.2 — Where you work

    You'll be working in Fred Meyer stores. Fred Meyer is a division of Kroger.

    Retail Odyssey serves other retailers, and their programs run differently. Everything in this document describes Fred Meyer work specifically. If you're ever scheduled outside Fred Meyer, ask me first — don't assume any of this carries over.

    1.3 — What the job actually is

    You are a Retail Reset Merchandiser. Typically once a week, a team goes into a store and rebuilds shelf sections to a new plan — new products in, discontinued products out, shelves moved, labels replaced. Sometimes, as the situation calls for it, we'll be in the same store multiple days in a week.

    The program that governs this work at Fred Meyer is called Kompass, and Section 2 explains it properly. It's the first thing to read after this page, because every other section assumes you know what it is.

    A few things worth knowing up front:

    • The work is physical. You're standing, lifting, and reaching for most of a shift.
    • The work is precise. A shelf is either set to the planogram or it isn't. There's no partial credit on a facing count.
    • The work is visible. The section you build is what customers shop for the next several months. That's the appeal of it, and it's also why the details matter.

    1.5 — How to use this document

    Before your first shift, read Sections 1 through 6. They cover what to wear, what to bring, how to log in, how to arrive, and how to work safely.

    Before your first reset, read the rest of Part I. It covers how to read a planogram, how to perform a reset, and how to close one out.

    Part II is reference, not reading. Don't work through it. It's there for when you meet something you don't recognize — a fixture, a term, a department that works differently. Glossary, special fixtures, storage, conditioning by department, front end, aisle navigation, bakery, shelf hardware, and the commodity list.

    The last section is a quick-reference card. Print it and keep it with your tools — the reset steps, the pull categories, and who to ask for what, on one page.

    Two sections are worth rereading after you've worked a shift or two, because they'll make more sense once you've seen the shelf: Section 2 (Kompass) and Section 7 (Reading a Planogram).

Section 4 — Professional Standards

    Section 4 — Professional Standards

    You represent Retail Odyssey inside someone else's store. To the customers around you, you're indistinguishable from staff — which means how you present affects both companies at once.

    Most of what follows is straightforward. The parts that surprise people are the phone rule, the bag rule, and the receipt rule, so read those closely.

    4.1 — What to wear

    Required on every assignment:

    Item Standard
    PoloCompany-issued. Wear it
    Name badgeYour Retail Odyssey badge, clipped to your shirt, name clearly visible
    PantsSolid blue, solid black, or plain khaki. No more than one inch above the ankle
    ShoesCompletely closed, clean, and skid-resistant

    Optional:

    • A solid-colored long-sleeve shirt under your polo
    • For refrigerated or frozen work: a solid-colored, logo-free sweater, sweatshirt, or jacket in blue, black, or gray

    On shoes — closed and skid-resistant isn't only about looking professional. You'll be on hard floors, around dropped product and spills, moving carts and hand trucks. This is a safety requirement wearing a dress-code hat.

    4.2 — What not to wear

    Pants and clothing:

    • Shorts, capri pants, sweatpants, yoga pants
    • Anything faded, torn, baggy, or tight
    • Colored patterns, logos, or writing

    Footwear:

    • Boots, with the exception of work boots

    Everything else:

    • Sunglasses
    • Headphones and music devices — see 4.4

    If you're unsure whether something you own meets the standard, ask me before your shift rather than finding out at the store.

    Appearance standards beyond the dress code above — anything this section doesn't cover — are in your employee handbook.

    4.3 — Grooming

    Neat, clean, and ready to work. That's the whole standard. You'll be within a few feet of customers for most of a shift, often reaching across them or working beside them in a narrow aisle.

    4.4 — Headphones and music devices

    Headphones are not allowed on the sales floor. Not any kind, not at any point during your assignment.

    This is the rule people test most, so here is exactly what it covers:

    • Both earbuds, or one earbud
    • Wired or wireless
    • Over-ear, on-ear, in-ear, bone conduction
    • Under a hood, under a hat, or under your hair
    • Playing nothing at all

    If it sits on or in your ear, it comes out before you walk onto the floor.

    Music devices are covered too, headphones or not. No speakers, no radios, no phone playing out loud — anywhere in the store, including the back room. The store controls the sound in its own building.

    Why this one isn't negotiable. You work in an aisle where carts, hand trucks, and pallet jacks move behind you. A customer trying to get past you, a teammate calling a warning, a store announcement, the sound of something falling — you need all of it. Every reset injury that starts with "I didn't hear it coming" starts here.

    The second reason is simpler: someone wearing headphones reads as unavailable. Customers won't approach you and store staff won't either, and part of your job is being approachable to both.

    Expect this to be enforced. Your lead will ask you to remove them. Repeated after that, it becomes a conversation with me. I'd rather it never gets that far, so treat this as settled before your first shift.

    4.5 — Phones

    Merchandisers: your personal phone stays on vibrate and out of sight for your entire assignment — including when you're walking through the store on a break.

    Leads: carry a phone for the Field App and for coordinating the team. That's a working tool, not an exception to the rule.

    When a phone does come out, three conditions hold for everyone:

    • On vibrate. Never on a ring tone, never on speaker
    • Business use only while you're in the store
    • Never while you're moving product. Stop, put down what you're carrying, then look at the phone. A hand on a phone is a hand not on the cart, the ladder, or the shelf you're loading

    A call you genuinely have to take is a call you take outside, on a break.

    Why it's written this way. A customer or store employee who sees you on a device reads it as you not working. They don't know whether you're checking a planogram or checking a text, and they don't stop to find out — they form an impression of Retail Odyssey and pass it to the store. That impression is the entire reason the rule exists.

    Practical version: check the Field App before your shift and after it. Not in the aisle. Section 3.4 covers the timing.

    4.6 — Personal items

    Purses, handbags, backpacks, and tote bags don't come into the store. Leave them locked in your vehicle.

    This isn't about trust. You're a non-employee moving through a building full of merchandise all day, and a bag you're carrying is a question nobody should have to ask you. Not carrying one means it never comes up.

    What you do bring — coat, lunch, tools — goes in the area your lead designates at the start of the shift. Ask where it is before you need it, not while you're holding it.

    4.7 — Eating, drinking, and purchases

    No food or drink on the sales floor. None, at any point. Not a coffee on a shelf, not a water bottle in a cart, not something in your hand while you walk. A drink set down on a shelf gets knocked into product, and a customer who sees you eating in an aisle sees a store that lets people eat in aisles.

    Eat before you arrive. Eating and drinking are permitted during lunch or a scheduled break, in the designated areas only. Ask your lead where those are on your first shift.

    If you buy something in the store during your shift:

    • Pay for it before you consume any part of it — food, drink, or product
    • Keep the receipt until the end of your shift
    • Be ready to show it to store staff on the way out, or any time you're asked

    That last one catches people. It isn't an accusation and it isn't personal — you're a non-employee carrying store merchandise, and the receipt is what makes that unremarkable. Keep it in a pocket, not in a cart you're going to return.

    4.8 — Customers

    Customers will mistake you for store staff. It happens constantly and it's a compliment to how you look.

    If someone asks you for help, help them if you can. Point them to the right aisle, hand them something off a high shelf, find them a store employee. If it's beyond what you can do, walk them to someone who can rather than sending them off with a direction.

    Be respectful of the store and everyone in it at all times. You're a guest with a badge.

    4.9 — Variations and accommodations

    Some dress guidelines vary by program. If you work an assignment with a different standard, you'll be told. If you're unsure which applies, ask me.

    If you need an accommodation for any reason, talk to me or to HR directly. Either route is fine, and you don't need to explain yourself to anyone else on the team.

Section 8 — Performing a Full Reset

    Section 8 — Performing a Full Reset

    A full reset rebuilds an entire category to a new planogram. Everything comes off, the shelves get reconfigured, and the category goes back up to the new plan.

    This section teaches the full reset end to end. Partial projects — NIIs and maintenances — are the same work applied to part of a set, and Section 8.6 covers what changes.

    Settle this first

    Does this store have digital shelf labels? Look at the shelf edge. Paper price tags, or small digital screens? Everything in this section is the same either way until Step 8, where the two split. Fred Meyer is partway through the rollout, so both kinds of store are live right now, and you can't assume from one visit to the next.

    8.1 — Rules that apply the entire time

    The aisle stays shoppable. Customers are shopping around you for the whole project. Keep your materials, baskets, and carts on one side so there's always a clear path through. This is not a courtesy — an aisle you've blocked is an aisle the store can't sell from. Where you can, work a bay at a time rather than opening the whole set at once.

    One cart in the aisle per person working. Two of you resetting cookies with four carts pulled means two carts in the aisle and two parked out of the way. Carts multiply faster than people do, and a cart nobody is standing at is just an obstacle.

    Shopping carts are ours to use, and only ours. Store policy is that vendors don't use shopping carts. Retail Odyssey has been granted an exception by Kroger corporate specifically for resets, so you are allowed. Expect occasional pushback from store associates who haven't heard about the exception — be polite, and tell your lead rather than arguing it out. Two things make the exception stick: never leave product sitting in a cart in the back room, and empty every cart and return it to the corral before you leave.

    The planogram is the authority — but the shelf is real. You set what the plan says, where it says, in the quantity it says. Where the fixture won't take it, you adjust facings on the shelf and call it out rather than stopping. Section 7.6 has the rule. What you don't do is redesign the set.

    Know which kind of mismatch you have. Footage off, shelf count off, wrong version or format — that's a planning problem, and you stop before you dismantle anything. Product that won't quite fit the facings it's given — that's a fitting problem, and you solve it and keep moving.

    Standing rule

    Refrigerated and frozen product gets 20 minutes out of the case. Longer than that and it goes into the walk-in cooler or freezer. Watch which one — refrigerated product in a freezer is a loss, and so is the reverse.

    Exception

    Bakery is 15 minutes, not 20. Bakery product is delicate and melts fast. See Part II, Bakery.

    8.2 — Before you touch product

    Work through this in order. Each step catches a problem that gets more expensive the longer it goes unnoticed.

    1. Pull your materials from the Kompass cart — Kompass Notes, planogram, strips, and tags
    2. Check the planogram version and effective date against what your lead is working from
    3. Read the Kompass Notes for this project — they tell you what's discontinued and flag any known discrepancies
    4. Confirm the section size matches the planogram — feet, doors, or inches, depending on what the plan calls for. Check the letter on the size code before you measure; a frozen set is counted in doors, not feet. Section 7.1
    5. Count the shelves and confirm they match the planogram
    6. Check whether new shelves have been supplied. If new shelving is available for this set, it goes in — old shelves get swapped, not reused
    7. Count your strips against the planogram. Do this before anything comes off the shelf — see below
    8. Stage your boxes — labeled and ready before anything comes off the shelf
    9. Locate the new-item stock in the back room so you're not hunting for it mid-set
    10. DSL stores only: confirm you have a Zebra. Leads, please ask the store for it early, before the shift starts. No Zebra by mid-shift, escalate to me — don't wait it out. I provide the login
    Stop

    Footage or shelf count doesn't match? Stop. Tell your lead before you dismantle anything. A wrong planogram caught now costs five minutes; caught halfway through a teardown, it costs the afternoon.

    Count the strips before you pull a single one

    This is the check people skip, and it's the one that leaves a set unfinishable.

    • Full reset: you should have a strip for every position on the planogram. Short by any, tell your lead before you start
    • Partial or NII: expect strips only for what's changing. That's normal and correct — but it means you pull only the strips you have replacements for

    The trap is on partials. The packet arrives with the planogram and a partial set of strips, which looks complete because a planogram came with it. Strip the whole set on that assumption and you've got positions with nothing to put back — and no way to fix it until another packet arrives.

    Match strips to positions first. Pull second.

    8.3 — The working order

    Three nested rules, and they never change:

    • Bay by bay, left to right — Bay 1 through Bay 5
    • Within a bay, bottom shelf to top — Shelf 1 upward
    • Within a shelf, position 1 outward — left to right

    Bottom to top matters more than it sounds. Product set on a higher shelf gets disturbed every time you adjust something below it. Working upward means you never rebuild the same shelf twice.

    If the set has both shelves and pegs, set the shelves bottom-up first, then hang the pegs top-down. Hanging pegs before the shelves are set means reaching over finished product, and something always comes off the hook.

    Complete one shelf before starting the next. To keep product off the floor, park items temporarily in open slots on shelves you haven't reached yet.

    8.4 — The steps

    1Step 1 — Pull discontinued items

    Before the teardown, clear out what's leaving.

    • Work from the Kompass Notes — they designate the DIs for this project
    • DIs carry closeout tags on the shelf edge; that's how you spot them
    • Route them to the store associate, or place them in clearly labeled boxes
    • Pull the tags with the items and keep them together

    You'll also find items the Notes don't list — damaged product, out-of-code product, and items not in the set at all. Those come off too, into their own labeled boxes. Part II covers the pull categories and where each one goes.

    Never take product home with you. Not damaged, not discontinued, not out of code. It goes to the store for disposition. This sounds obvious right up until someone hands you a dented box and says "just take it."

    2Step 2 — Strip what has to move, and clean it

    You don't empty the whole set. You empty what has to move.

    A shelf whose product isn't changing position and whose height isn't changing doesn't come off. Stripping it anyway costs you the time to take it down and put it back, and every handling is a chance to damage something or set it back wrong.

    Strip a shelf when:

    • The shelf height is changing
    • Product on it is moving position, or coming off the set
    • The shelf itself is being swapped for a new one
    • The shelf edge needs work — rail, channel, or strip being replaced

    Leave it when none of those apply. Same principle as 8.6: touch what changed.

    The DSL guidance says the same thing about tags — slide and re-associate rather than pulling the lot off. Product, tags, strips: on this job the instinct is always to move what moves and leave the rest alone.

    When you do strip a shelf:

    • The tag travels with the product. Pull the tag with its item and stick it to the last piece of that product in the cart. Whoever sets it back then has a tag in hand the moment they pick that item up
    • Load the cart from the handle end, left to right, snaked up to the top, like items touching. If you walk away, the next person can pick up exactly where you stopped without guessing
    • Wipe down the shelf and the tag molding with the cleaning supplies from the Kompass cart
    • New shelves get cleaned too if they've been sitting in storage

    Clean every shelf you moved, and its molding — not just the ones that looked dirty. You won't have a better opportunity, because that shelf is never emptier than it is right now. A shelf you didn't need to strip doesn't need cleaning either.

    3Step 3 — Set the shelf height

    • Read the height for this shelf from the planogram's position table
    • Measure from the top of the shelf to the top of the next shelf up — the rule is printed on every planogram page
    • Read the tape at eye level, head-on, the way you'd read a measuring cup. Looking down at it from an angle reads long, and across six shelves that drift adds up
    • If the shelf won't sit at the exact height using the nearest hole, leave too much space rather than too little. Too little and the product doesn't fit at all
    • Bread and chips sit on wire shelves — measure to where the product sits, not to the top of the front lip. The front edge of a wire shelf is not its deck
    • Adjust the lowest shelf first if it needs to move, then work upward
    • Move a single shelf yourself. Anything deeper than 15 inches takes two people, and steel beams, wood shelves, and shelving structures always take help

    If your heights close correctly against the fixture height, you measured right. Section 7.3 walks through that check.

    4Step 4 — Set the shelf edge

    Shelf-edge hardware goes in with the shelf empty. It's far harder once product is in front of you.

    Every store:

    • Check the data skins. They're the clear channel guides snapped to the front edge. Damaged, missing, or wrong size, tell your lead — a strip has nowhere to go without one
    • Pull the existing strips — but only the ones you have replacements for. You counted them in 8.2
    • Install the new strips per the planogram. The planogram is what tells you which strip goes where — the Kompass Notes tell you what's changing, not where the strips land

    DSL stores, one addition. The shelf edge carries an ESL rail, and the channel snaps into it. Check the rail before the strip goes in — a bent or missing rail means the tag has nowhere to sit. The parts reference in Part II will tell you which one you need and how to ask for it.

    Order of assembly

    Rail, then channel or data strip, then planogram strip, then tag. Never a tag before a strip. The tag gets matched to the strip during association, so a tag installed first has nothing to match to and comes back off.

    The channel data strips still have the slot behind them for the travel strip to slide into. Nothing changes about how you handle travel strips in a DSL store.

    A full reset replaces every strip on the set.

    5Step 5 — Set the category to the planogram

    • Place each product at its position on the shelf, working left to right
    • Set the facing count exactly as the Fac column reads — the number of that product side by side across the front
    • Align product between the black lines on the strip. Those lines are the position boundaries
    • All labels face forward
    • Fill front to back behind each facing as depth allows
    • When the product description and the UPC disagree, scan the UPC. Descriptions are abbreviated and repeat across similar items
    • When the strip's facing count doesn't match the planogram's, look one bay over. The planogram is written bay by bay; the strip runs the full length of the run. A product at the end of a shelf usually continues into the next bay, and the strip is showing you the combined total. Section 7.7 has the detail

    If the facings don't fit, take the product down by one facing and keep setting. No room for even one facing, borrow a facing from a neighbor on the same shelf that has several, so the item still lands somewhere. Tell your lead either way — but don't stop the set over it. Section 7.6 covers both cases.

    If a product isn't in stock, hold its space with a dummy facing — a same-size item turned backwards in the slot. Backwards is the point: nobody buys it by mistake, the gap stays the right width, and whoever fills it later doesn't have to re-measure. Tell your lead so the store knows there's a hole.

    Get a facing count wrong and everything to the right of it lands in the wrong place. Verify as you go rather than at the end.

    6Step 6 — Rotate the stock

    Older product to the front, newer product to the back of each facing. This is proper Key Retailing rotation and it applies to every facing you set.

    It's the step most often skipped, and it matters: product left sitting behind newer stock ages out and becomes a write-off for the store. A shelf that's perfectly set with reversed rotation is a shelf that generates out-of-code product in a few weeks — and it will look correct to anyone walking it.

    7Step 7 — Bring up the new-item stock

    • Pull all new-item stock from the back room onto the shelf
    • New items are flagged in the planogram's position tables and listed on the planogram's NEW page
    • Perishable and frozen new items have a designated location — if it came out of a fridge or freezer, it goes back into one

    8Step 8 — Tag the set

    This is where the two kinds of store split. Look at the shelf edge and pick the path.

    One rule applies in both, so learn it once. If a position has no tag:

    1. Check for a shelf strip. If there's a strip, the strip stands in. Don't hand-write anything and don't improvise a replacement
    2. Highlight that position on the planogram
    3. Give the planogram to your lead, who passes it to the store's price changer

    Where a set runs without strips at all, hand-write a tag so the product isn't sitting unlabeled — then highlight and hand it up the same way. What you never do is leave a position with nothing on it and say nothing about it. A highlighted planogram is how the store finds out; your memory at the end of a shift is not.

    8A — Paper tag stores

    • Tags come from the store's price changer, staged ahead of the visit. Some stores call the same person the scan or pricing coordinator
    • Place each tag to the left of its product
    • Hang every tag provided
    • Missing a tag? Follow the rule above — strip stands in, highlight the planogram, hand it to your lead

    8B — Digital shelf label stores

    In a DSL store the digital label replaces the paper tag. You are not hanging both.

    Where DSL goes

    • Center Store grocery
    • Checklanes
    • Coolers — magnet mount
    • Parts of produce, not all of it
    Where it does not currently go

    Freezers · Frozen · General Merchandise · Floral · Service Departments · Fuel Center · Freestanding Displays · Wine · Tobacco · Seasonal · American Greetings · Gift Cards · Clip Strips · Lobby · Dump Bins · Furniture

    General Merchandise stays on UPLs and is worked as normal. Paper tags, no change.

    This list is current, not permanent. Check with your lead if you're unsure.

    Pick the right size — the number is printed on the shelf strip.

    SizeWhere
    1.5The smallest tag. Tight barcode space
    2.6The majority size throughout the store
    4.2 / 4.5Large format. Produce bins and similar fixtures
    MagnetsCoolers only. No magnet available, use the magnetic adapter or the alternative in the parts reference

    Match the tag to the number on the strip. If it covers another barcode, size DOWN. A label that hides a barcode stops the shelf being scannable, which defeats the point of having it.

    Associating a tag

    1. Open the DSL app — Assign DSL / Match DSL
    2. Scan the DSL tag. The clicker at the bottom right fires the beam
    3. Scan the shelf strip barcode, or the product
    4. Watch for the flash. That's what confirms the link
    5. Snap the tag into place, then move on

    Scan the DSL first, then the barcode. That order is what links them.

    Take the old paper tag down only after the screen has flashed. Not before. If the association fails and you've already pulled the paper, the position has nothing on it at all.

    Snapping it in. Go in from the bottom — pop the tag toward the top, then pop the bottom in. There are clips along the bottom edge, and the snap is loud and obvious when it seats correctly. If you didn't hear it, it isn't in.

    Holders you'll meet: J-2 skins, which are clip-on; the dairy, meat and dry holder that turns up across most of those sets; and the produce holder. Part II has the full parts reference.

    When product moves

    • Move the tag with the product
    • Tags from confirmed deletes can be reused for new items
    • No tags left on empty spaces
    Don't do this

    Don't strip a whole set of tags and re-hang them.

    Slide the tag to where it now belongs and re-associate it to the product that's there. You don't have to take a tag off the rail to change what it's pointing at, and you don't have to disassociate it first — just associate it to the new product.

    Pulling every tag off a set and starting again is hours of work to reach the same place. It also puts a pile of loose tags on your cart, and loose tags are how deletes end up un-disassociated.

    Disassociating

    You do not have to disassociate a tag before associating a new one. Skip straight to associating.

    What must be disassociated is every tag from a delete, at end of set. Open the app, select Disassociate, scan the tag, confirm. Unused tags go back on the Kompass cart.

    Leftovers break re-association. A tag that's still associated to something deleted can't be cleanly picked up by the next set. This is the reason the end-of-set sweep isn't optional.

    Removal tools

    Removal tools live on the Kompass cart. They're ours, not the store's — don't leave them behind.

    Tags come off without tools and won't break. A putty knife or a canoe-clip tool works; the flat edge is what matters.

    If you can't get a Zebra

    Your supervisor provides the login. The login is tied to the store — there's no store to select, and multiple team leads can be logged in at different stores at the same time.

    Leads: request the Zebra from the store early AM, before the shift starts. No Zebra by mid-shift, escalate the same day. Don't argue with the store — escalation handles it.

    Escalation path

    Team Lead reports immediately → me → Kompass Manager.

    9Step 9 — Verify

    Before you call the shelf done:

    • Every tag matched to the product it belongs to
    • Facing counts match the Fac column
    • Shelf heights match the planogram
    • Rotation is correct — older forward
    • Labels facing forward, product aligned between the strip's black lines
    • Nothing from the pull categories is still sitting on the shelf

    DSL stores, four more:

    • Correct tag size on every position
    • Tags moved with their product
    • Every delete disassociated
    • Unused tags back on the Kompass cart — along with the removal tools

    Worth knowing: finished sets get checked against the planogram by Store Intelligence, which pairs AI with our planograms to confirm the set matches the plan. A set that looks finished but isn't will get flagged after you've left. Verifying now is faster than coming back.

    10Step 10 — Close out the set

    • DSL stores: every delete disassociated, every unused tag and removal tool back on the Kompass cart. Leftovers break re-association for whoever works this set next, and the removal tools are ours
    • Move backstock to the designated store associate, or into labeled overstock boxes
    • Clear the area of clutter and trash
    • Return boxes, crates, and any borrowed equipment to the store receiver
    • Empty and return every shopping cart to the corral
    • Leave the aisle better than you found it

    8.6 — What changes on a Partial

    NIIs and maintenances use the same mechanics on part of a set. The discipline is restraint — you touch what changed and nothing else.

    StepFull ResetPartial
    Discontinued itemsPull all DIs per the NotesSame
    StripsReplace every strip on the set — confirm you have a full set firstYou'll get strips only for what's changing. Pull only what you can replace
    SettingSet the entire category to the new planogramSet only the changing area to the new strips
    No strips in the category—Cut the new items into the set per the planogram
    StrippingStrip only the shelves that have to moveSame — and far fewer of them
    CleaningWipe all moved shelving and tag moldingsWipe only the affected shelves
    Tags — paperHang all provided, hand-write as neededSame
    Tags — DSLTag and associate the whole setTag and associate the changed positions; disassociate anything removed
    BackstockTo the associate or labeled boxesSame
    AisleShoppable throughoutSame

    The temptation on a partial is to tidy up neighboring shelves while you're there. Don't. Work outside the changing area isn't tracked, isn't signed off, and can put a shelf out of sync with a planogram someone else is working from.

    8.7 — Departments that work differently

    Bakery runs its own version of a Kompass reset — a different way of reading the planogram, black ID tags under product, magnetic shelf strips, POG books on clear hooks, and a separate completion standard. Read the bakery section in Part II before your first bakery reset. Don't work one off this section alone.

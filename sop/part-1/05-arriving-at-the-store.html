Section 5 — Arriving at the Store

    Section 5 — Arriving at the Store

    The first twenty minutes of a shift set up everything after it. Arrive on time, check in with the right person, and get your materials to the aisle in one organized trip or two — and the reset starts clean.

    5.1 — Getting there

    Check the Field App the night before. It gives you the store, your start time, your lead's name, and an estimated drive time.

    Treat that estimate as a floor, not a plan. It doesn't know about traffic, weather, or the fact that you'll need time to park and walk in. Add margin.

    Arriving on time matters more here than in most jobs. A reset team works as a unit — your lead assigns projects at check-in, and one person arriving late holds up the assignment for everyone else, not just themselves.

    If you're going to be late, tell your lead as early as you know. Not when you arrive.

    5.2 — Parking

    Park near the back of the lot.

    The spaces closest to the store belong to customers. This is one of the easiest things to get right and one of the most visible things to get wrong — store management notices vendor vehicles in the good spots, and they remember.

    Factor the walk from the back of the lot into your arrival time.

    Your lead or I will tell you how to get in. Don't guess and don't default to the front doors.

    It's usually the grocery receiving door — the loading dock at the back where the semis back up to unload. Beside the dock there's a normal person-sized door, called the man door. That's the one drivers use, and it's the one we use.

    No one comes with you. Not children, not family, not a friend waiting inside. The store is a workplace and you're there as a vendor — anyone who isn't working the project doesn't come in.

    5.3 — Checking in

    You check in with your lead. They'll be at the front of the store.

    Your lead checks in with the store — with the Kompass Champion or, more often, the Food Manager. That's their job, not yours. You don't need to find store management on arrival.

    Your lead will:

    • Give you any last-minute instructions
    • Assign you to your project for the day
    • Walk you through the store if you're new to it
    • Introduce you to the store staff you'll need
    • Have you grab a shopping cart for your equipment and materials

    If something looks wrong — a planogram that doesn't match, missing materials, a section that isn't what the assignment says — tell your lead. They route it from there. Discrepancies go up through the lead to me, not directly from you to store management.

    5.4 — Who's who in the store

    You'll deal with three store roles regularly. Knowing which one to ask saves a lot of walking.

    Who What they handle You'll need them for

    Price changer (also called the scan or pricing coordinator)Shelf tags, pricing, shelf stripsMissing tags, tags that don't match, replacement strips. A position you couldn't tag goes to your lead, who brings it here

    Your lead is still your first call. This table is so you understand who does what, not an instruction to go around your lead. When your lead sends you to the scan coordinator for a missing tag, this is why.

    5.5 — Getting your materials to the aisle

    Your working materials come from the Kompass cart — Kompass Notes, planograms, strips, and tags for the week's projects. Your lead will point you to it.

    For everything else, the receiver is the source: new-item stock, boxes and crates, and a hand truck, dolly, or U-boat if you need one to move product.

    Loading:

    • Put your equipment in the cart first, then your materials on top of it
    • Don't overload. Make as many trips as it takes
    • Use a hand truck or dolly for anything heavy rather than stacking the cart high

    Multiple trips are not inefficiency. An overloaded cart is how product gets damaged, how equipment gets dropped, and how someone gets hurt navigating a corner. The trip back to the aisle costs a minute; a broken case costs more than that.

    If something you need isn't there — no strips, no cleaning supplies, no boxes — tell your lead. Don't improvise around it and don't buy it yourself.

    5.6 — Before you start setting

    Once you're at your section with your materials, Section 8.2 picks up: check the planogram version and effective date, read the Kompass Notes, measure the section, count the shelves, and stage your boxes.

    Do that before anything comes off a shelf.

    Section 3 — Before Your First Shift

    Two things to handle before you show up: get your tools, and get into the Field App. Neither takes long, and both are difficult to sort out on the morning of your first assignment.

    3.1 — Your tool kit

    You supply these yourself. They're yours, you bring them to every assignment, and you're responsible for keeping them in working condition.

    Tool What it's for
    Tape measure, 12 ft or longerShelf heights and section footage. A short tape covers the heights but won't measure a 20-foot section, and confirming footage is the first check you make on any set. Get one that's easy to read
    Screwdrivers — Phillips and flat headShelf hardware, brackets, and pegboard mounting
    PliersStubborn clips, bent hardware, and anything a screwdriver won't turn
    Box cutterOpening boxes of new items
    Spare bladesDull blades are a hazard, not an inconvenience. Replace them often
    Safety glovesRequired for frozen resets, and any time the store requires them
    ScraperRemoving old tags and stubborn dirt from shelves and moldings
    Permanent markerLabeling boxes and hand-writing shelf tags
    PenNotes, and anything the marker is too heavy for
    HighlighterMarking positions on the planogram. When you can't tag a position, you highlight it and hand the planogram to your lead — Section 8, Step 8
    ScissorsTrimming strips, opening packaging a blade would tear
    Scotch tapeTemporary fixes on strips and tags until the real replacement arrives

    Strongly recommended, not required:
    Knee pads or a kneeling matYou set bottom shelf first and work up, so you start every bay on the floor. Six bays into a shift, this is the difference between a good day and a bad back. Get them before your first assignment rather than after the shift that convinces you

    A box cutter, not a pocketknife. No folding knives, no switch-style knives, nothing that isn't a purpose-built retractable box cutter. A blade you can't retract in the half-second before you set it down is a blade that finds someone, and a pocketknife in a store also reads very differently to a customer than a box cutter does.

    None of this is expensive. The whole kit is available cheaply, and it's yours for as long as you look after it.

    3.2 — Setting up the Field App

    The Field App is where your schedule lives. Get into it before your first shift, not during it.

    The address is prod.sasretail.com/en/field.

    Yes, it says SAS. Section 1.1 covers why. It's the right address.

    You'll also hear it called Retail Logic or the SAS Retail App — that's the same tool under its official names. Most people just say the Field App.

    It runs in a browser on any web-enabled device — computer, tablet, or phone. Bookmark it. You'll open it constantly and typing that URL gets old fast.

    What it does for you:

    • Shows your schedule and any new assignments
    • Tells you which store you're assigned to
    • Gives you your lead's name
    • Estimates your drive time to the store
    • Carries any special instructions for your assignment

    Log in every day. Assignments get added, and special instructions post there. Checking daily is the difference between knowing where you're going tomorrow and finding out tomorrow.

    3.3 — Logging in

    Your company ID

    Your login is your official company ID. It's typically formatted firstname.lastname@youradv.com — the "youradv" part is Your Advantage.

    Not every ID follows that pattern, so variations do occur. Yours is in your welcome email — use exactly what it gives you.

    How much of it to type

    You'll hit more than one login screen, and they don't all want the same thing. Look at the screen you're on:

    If the login screen says Type
    MicrosoftThe whole thing — firstname.lastname@youradv.com
    Advantage, SAS, or Retail OdysseyJust firstname.lastname — no @youradv.com

    That one detail accounts for most failed logins. If you're getting rejected with credentials you know are right, check which screen you're looking at before you do anything else.

    Your password

    It's the same password you set up with Oracle during onboarding. Not a separate one, not one that came in an email — the Oracle password.

    Getting your credentials

    Your login information comes to your personal email — the address you gave HR. Watch for a welcome message once your onboarding steps are complete. It contains the link and your username.

    Didn't get it? Check spam first — it's usually there. Still nothing, contact me and I'll get it to you.

    3.4 — A note on phones

    You need a device to reach the Field App, and for most people that's a phone. That's fine — but understand how the two rules fit together.

    Check the app before your shift, not during it. On the sales floor, personal phones stay silenced and out of sight. Leads carry a phone for the app and for coordination; merchandisers don't. Section 4 covers this properly.

    So: look at your schedule and instructions in the morning, or the night before. Confirm your hours at the end of the day, after your shift. Not in the aisle.

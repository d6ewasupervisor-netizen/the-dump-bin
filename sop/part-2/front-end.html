Front End

    Part II — Front End

    The front end is the checklanes, the self-checkouts, and the service desk. It's the last thing a customer passes and the busiest floor space in the building, and it's set differently enough from an aisle to need its own rules.

    Three things make it different:

    • Everything is described from the customer's point of view, not yours
    • The fixtures are small, and the hardware is specific to them — 6-inch pegs, tag holders, tiny racks bolted to machines
    • You are working in the one part of the store that never really closes

    Customer left, customer right

    Stand in the lane, facing the front of the store — the way a customer moves through it. Now left is customer left and right is customer right.

    Every checklane and self-checkout merchandiser is identified this way, on the planogram and in conversation. It does not change based on which way you happen to be standing while you work.

    Get this backwards and you'll build a mirror image of the planogram, correctly, and have to strip it. Orient yourself before you start, not after.

    Checklanes

    Tails

    The tail is the part of a manned checklane where product is merchandised — the merchandiser unit running alongside the belt. You'll see it written as a size on the planogram:

    Size
    MEDMedium tail
    LGLarge tail
    XLExtra large

    So a planogram headed MED TAIL CHECKLANE is telling you which physical unit it's built for. Check that against the lane in front of you before you set anything — lanes in the same store aren't all the same size.

    Match the planogram to the setup

    Checklanes aren't uniform. Before you start, confirm the planogram matches what's actually in the lane:

    • Cooler orientation — rear-facing or side-facing
    • Snack configuration
    • KPF — Kroger Personal Finance, the gift card and telecom fixtures

    A planogram built for a side-facing cooler won't set onto a rear-facing one, and finding that out at position 1 is much cheaper than finding out at position 20.

    Peg hardware

    • Checklanes use 6-inch peg hooks. Not the 11-inch pegs used on aisle peg sets
    • Every hook needs a clear plastic tag holder
    • Every hook needs an inventory control clip, with the product pulled forward and held at the front of the hook by the ICC

    That combination — 6-inch hook, tag holder, ICC — is the standard checklane peg. A hook missing any of the three isn't finished.

    Self-checkouts

    SCOs are set the same way as manned lanes and laid out differently in every store.

    The two layouts

    Layout
    6-packA group of six machines — three on customer left, three on customer right
    Bullpen / corralA larger group arranged around the perimeter of an area, with one entrance and one exit

    Which one a store has changes how you walk the set and how many merchandisers you're working with. Look at the layout before you start rather than assuming it matches the last store.

    The fixtures

    Candy and gift card racks sit around each machine, and they're identified by the model of the machine — Toshiba SCO-6, Toshiba SCO-7, and so on. The model determines which racks fit. If a rack won't mount, check the machine model against the planogram before you force anything.

    SCO coolers carry merchandising racks too. They're either single or double rear-facing coolers, and the planogram will specify which.

    Tobacco cases

    Tobacco is a locked, regulated set, and it has two rules that don't apply anywhere else in the store.

    Where the cases are

    Case Where
    Short casesUsually by the self-checkouts or the customer service desk
    Tall casesAlong the front wall, occasionally behind the customer service desk
    Fuel center casesThe fuel center has its own

    The state rule

    Different states have different tobacco planograms. Confirm the planogram you're holding is for the correct state before you touch the case.

    This is the one place in the building where setting the wrong plan isn't just wrong, it's a regulatory problem. Check it every time, even in a store you've set before.

    Setting the case

    • Verify shelf heights first, same as any reset
    • Work from the bottom of the case to the top
    • Cigarettes go in 4- or 5-pusher sections that lock into the case shelves
    • Smokeless tobacco uses its own pushers — rounded front, and they lock into the shelf the same way
    • Anything not in a pusher is stocked front to back

    Digital shelf labels at the front end

    Checklanes are explicitly in the rollout. They're one of the four places DSL currently goes, and the parts are specific to them — the checkout hardware family in the parts reference, including the wire fence locking holder and the suspension hook with button clips.

    Everything else at the front end is currently out. Tobacco, gift cards, clip strips, seasonal, American Greetings, and the service departments all sit outside the rollout for now. So do freestanding displays and the fuel center.

What Counts as a Display

Primary versus secondary, and the test that settles it

Primary versus secondary
Primary location
Where the item normally lives: its planogrammed shelf, its bunker, its dedicated fixture. Never scanned.
Secondary location
Anywhere else the item appears while also stocked in its home location. Scanned.
One scan per unique UPC
  • Unique means each flavor, size, or variety.
  • Six identical Doritos displays across the front of the store is one scan, not six.
  • The same item is scanned once per location it appears in.
  • Two packs of ribs priced differently by weight are one UPC. Scan one.
The two-question test
  1. Is this a secondary display?A shipper, endcap, table, or any placement that is not the item’s home location.
  2. Is it stocked elsewhere?Can these items also be found in a primary or home location somewhere in this store?
Yes to bothScan the display.
When the rules do not fit
  • Ask a nearby store employee whether the item is stocked elsewhere. If yes, scan it.
  • If the item is stocked nowhere else in the store, this is its primary location. Leave it.
When in doubt, scan the displayAn extra scan is acceptable. A missed display is not.

Items Never Scanned

These are never captured, in any location

Fixtures and areas
  • The checkout and check lanes
  • Shelf extenders
  • Clip-strips
  • Trial size bins
  • Items on countertops
  • Carbonated beverage coolers
Product conditions
  • Damaged or manager clearance items
  • Red sticker reduced-price items
  • A black line through the UPC
  • A sticker placed over the UPC
Categories
  • Ice
  • Eyewear and glasses
  • Promo dishes, pans, rugs
  • General Merchandise outside Grocery GM
Scanned only when in a shipper
  • Greeting cards
  • Toys, especially Hot Wheels
  • Trial size products
  • Books and magazines
  • Film and cameras
  • Cosmetics
  • Clothing
  • DVDs
Clearance marksA blacked-out UPC, a covered barcode, or a red clearance sticker marks product being cleared, not promoted. Do not scan it.

Displays Not to Scan

Primary locations and out-of-scope areas

Common ones on nearly every route
Promo tables with black stickers
A black label sticker marks a planogrammed item. The table is a primary location.
Permanent branded fixtures
Krispy Kreme racks and Tastykake fixtures are the home location for those items.
Bakery cases and cake displays
Primary location. Promo items sitting on top of the case are scanned, under Other.
Refrigerated meat and bunker walls
Primary location, even under large promotional pricing signage.
Themed power wings at home
An ice-cream-themed power wing in the ice cream aisle is a primary location.
Battery centers
The battery endcap is primary. Batteries in shippers, on other endcaps, or on power wings are scanned.
Garden Centers
Nothing inside is scanned. The same applies to any separate non-grocery section, except Adult Beverage.
General Merchandise
Out of scope. Grocery GM such as lightbulbs, charcoal, and fire starter logs is in scope.

Endcap or In Aisle?

The most common location question in the program

Ask yourselfDid you walk down into the aisle, past the endcap, to reach the display?
If no — the location is End Caps
  • You reached it without walking past an endcap
  • Includes power wings, tie-ins, and stacked product beside the endcap
  • An endcap-style fixture set halfway up an aisle is still an endcap if you did not pass an endcap to reach it. Judge by the walk, not the fixture.
If yes — the location is In Aisle
  • The sales floor inside the aisle, primary shelving to the left and right
  • Often includes Produce, Bakery, and Deli down the perimeter aisles
  • Before filing as In Aisle, confirm it is not part of an endcap, or in the store front or store rear.

Photo or Hand Scan?

Two ways to add a UPC, and the places only one of them works

Photo is fine here
  • Corrugated shippers and floor displays
  • Endcaps with standard shelving
  • Front of store and rear of store displays
  • In-shelf promotional sections
  • Seasonal floor shippers
Hand scan only, no exceptions
  • All Produce items
  • All Meat and Seafood items
  • Every table display
  • Three-tier baskets and rounder racks
  • Refrigerated and bunker displays, prepared meals, and bakery items
Hand scanning, step by step
  1. Open manual scanUse the blue arrow.
  2. Scan BarcodeCamera opens in barcode mode.
  3. Hold the line over the barcodeThe UPC is added automatically.
  4. Check the countTotal shows in Product List.

Photo Guidelines

Frame it the way the technology needs to read it

Do
  • Get all four corners of the display in the frame
  • Stand about six feet back, and shoot straight on
  • One section, about four feet wide, per photo
  • At least one front label per item facing forward
  • Move a large price sign that blocks product, then shoot
Do not
  • Shoot at an angle. Photos must be straight on.
  • Stand close. The system may read the wrong part of the image.
  • Cover a whole long display in one shot
  • Remerchandise the display to make it photogenic
  • Worry about overhead signage or extra space in frame
Correct photo examples
One front label is enoughDo not reface the whole display. If front-facing would take a lot of work, hand scan the unique items instead.

When a Photo Will Not Work

The four failures that come back flagged

What fails, and what to do
Shot at an angle
Reposition and shoot straight on. If that is impossible, hand scan each unique item.
Taken too close
Step back to about six feet. Too close, and the system can read a pictured item as a real one.
Sign blocking product
Move the sign, take the photo, put the sign back.
Corner cut off
Use the redo arrow and reframe all four corners.
Resolve flagged images before you end your shiftThe app flags blurry photos, duplicates, partial coverage, and wrong angle. Retake or remove each one, then complete the task and select the reason where the app asks for it.
Leave the app openStay logged in and leave the app running after you finish, until the remaining photos and UPCs finish uploading.

Produce

Walk it through. Hand scan and PLU entry only.

No photos in the Produce departmentEver.
Where Produce displays are filed
  • End Caps at the end of the produce aisles
  • In Aisle in line with the front endcaps, and anything in Produce not on a table
  • Tables for produce tables that are not the home location
  • Never Front or Rear of Store, even when the Produce section sits at the front or back of the store.
Three ways an item comes in
  1. Standard UPC barcodeHand scan it. Common on bagged apples and grapes, and on watermelons, honeydews, and pumpkins.
  2. Sticker with a stacked barcodeDo not scan it. Type the 4 or 5 digit PLU from the sticker into the barcode field.
  3. No sticker at allUse the sale price sign. Check the description matches, then type the last 4 or 5 digits of the UPC after the leading zeros.
Never scan the barcode on a sales signRead the digits and type them in. The barcode on shelf and sale signage is not the product UPC.

Entering a PLU Manually

Seven taps, in the Survey step of the task

  1. Go to the Survey stepInside the open task.
  2. Tap Scan BarcodeAt the bottom of the screen.
  3. Turn off the cameraUse the Turn off camera control.
  4. Tap into Or enter UPC codeThe field at the top of the list.
  5. Type the PLUThe 4 or 5 digit number, in the text box.
  6. Tap Look upNext to the field.
  7. Check the confirmationAdded appears and your entry shows in the list.
Two ways a Produce item comes inA UPC you can hand scan, or a 4 to 5 digit PLU you type into the barcode field. Nothing in Produce is ever photographed.

Adult Beverage, Drug and Grocery GM

Three areas with rules of their own

Adult Beverage
  • Designated Adult Beverage sections are in scope, including separate store-front Adult Beverage stores
  • There is no separate Adult Beverage task. File Adult Beverage displays into the location that matches where they sit: endcaps, in-aisle, front, rear.
  • The Adult Beverage section usually comes late in the route. Reopen completed tasks as needed to add its displays.
Drug and Grocery GM
  • Free-standing promotional fixtures in Drug/GM are scanned
  • Seasonal health displays such as suntan, cough, cold, and flu are scanned when those items are also found elsewhere in the store
  • Grocery GM is in scope: lightbulbs, charcoal, fire starter logs stocked in the grocery aisles
  • GM outside Grocery GM is not: grills, patio furniture, storage bins, birdhouses
